About · 01

Who We Are

We are a group of people who care about learning as a lived experience, not an abstraction. We have watched students study for hours and feel no closer to understanding. We have watched parents invest their savings in preparation, only to watch their children struggle on exam day. So we asked ourselves a simple question: why is this still so hard?

PrepHooks is our answer. It is a system that pays attention to how a student performs, shows them precisely where to work, and guides their progress with honesty and clarity—nothing more, nothing less.

We are not educators who built a product. We are product people who built a learning system. That distinction shapes everything we do.

About · 02

Why We Started

We started PrepHooks because we noticed something troubling.

For decades, exam preparation has changed its tools without changing its approach. Printed past questions became digital question banks. Question banks became apps. But the student is still handed a mountain of content and left to work out, alone, what to study, when to study it, and whether they are making progress.

Meanwhile, the world around that student changed completely. Attention itself became contested ground—fragmented, pulled in a dozen directions, shaped by the device in every pocket. The preparation system never moved to meet it.

We started PrepHooks because we believed there was a better way. Not a more complicated way. Not a more technological way. A more human way.

About · 03

What We Observed

The more we looked, the more we saw a pattern.

Students were not failing because they lacked access to content. Past questions have existed for decades. Textbooks are everywhere. Search engines and language models have made answers almost effortless to find. Content was never the problem.

Students were not failing because they lacked effort. We met students who spent hours each day with their noses in books, who sat through tutorial centers, who gave up sleep and time with friends—and still walked into the exam hall uncertain of their readiness.

The gap was not in what they had. The gap was in how they studied.

Most students default to methods that feel productive but are not: re-reading notes, highlighting text, cramming the night before. These create the illusion of understanding—familiarity mistaken for knowledge.

This pattern has been documented for decades. It persists because studying well is not intuitive. It has to be built into the experience, made simple enough that the student can spend their attention on learning rather than on figuring out how to learn.

About · 04

What We Decided to Do

We decided to build a tool that would close that gap.

Not by adding more content. Not by dressing up the same old approach with new features. By building a system designed around how learning actually happens.

We built a loop:

Teach — Clear, bite-sized lessons that build understanding without overwhelming the mind.

Test — Questions that target exactly what needs attention, based on how the student has performed before.

Track — A visual map of progress, honest and unsparing, so the student always knows where they stand.

We made the experience feel like a conversation, not a transaction. One question at a time. One swipe at a time. No dashboards to interpret, no decisions to make before the real work starts. Just a clear, quiet path forward.

About · 05

What We Believe

We believe effective preparation should be straightforward and honest.

A score that flatters a student is a score that fails them. We believe real improvement starts with an accurate picture of where a student stands today, not where they wish they were.

We believe a learning tool should get out of the way. It should not compete for attention. It should not demand that a student learn how to use it before they can learn anything else. It should be simple, focused, and quiet, so the work that matters gets the student's full attention.

We believe personalization is not a feature. It is a necessity. Every student's strengths are different. Every student's gaps are different. Every student learns at their own pace. A system that treats everyone the same helps no one as well as it could.
